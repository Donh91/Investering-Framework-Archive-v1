# Lead/Lag Analysis

## Result

Historical lead-time is not estimable for any recovered sensor because Stage 1 reconstructed zero exact eligible historical event states.

The preregistered horizons remain frozen and must be preserved prospectively:

`+6h, +12h, +24h, +48h, +72h, +7d, +14d, +30d`.

No horizon is selected post hoc.

## Sensor-family implications

### Early Rotation Pre-Trigger
Its stated purpose is early-window detection before classical confirmation. That makes lead-time its central prospective claim. A valid forward test must measure whether the composite triggers earlier than ETH/BTC, breadth and BTC.D baselines, while also measuring false starts.

### Fake Rotation Type 3
This is better framed as a veto/filter after an early-window attempt than as an independent predictor. Relevant lag is time from Pre-Trigger/brief ETH-BTC improvement to failed follow-through. This cannot be estimated historically from the recovered sample.

### ODM
ODM's strongest defensible role is methodological: report all frozen horizons and learn prospectively which families mature slowly or quickly. It may describe maturation differences after sufficient new sample, but may not retrospectively relabel an old miss as a success by choosing a favorable horizon.

### ETF-era divergence family
BTC and ecosystem outcomes must be measured separately at each horizon. A BTC-survival outcome cannot substitute for alt-market health.

## Conclusion

The archive supports lead/lag hypotheses, not lead/lag statistics. Any numeric lead-time claim from the legacy material would exceed the evidence.
