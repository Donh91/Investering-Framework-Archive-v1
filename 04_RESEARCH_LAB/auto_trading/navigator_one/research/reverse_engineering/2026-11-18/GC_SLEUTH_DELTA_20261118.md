### 5.1 Newly inspected independent sources and design corrections
- S1 https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22261 (Manufactured Authenticity, preprint available 17 Nov 2026; full rendered methods 3.1–3.4, results 4.2–4.3 and limitations 5.4 inspected). Observational dataset 996,834 launches, 50,895 creator wallets; holder sample 4,000 stratified tokens (1,000/cluster). Content-template cohort reply incidence 71.6%, bonding-curve completion 1.10%, compared with 69.7% and 1.46% for organic-style cohort; high-velocity cohort 64.9% and 3.24%. Labels describe behavior, not proven identity or bot intent. Authors identify discrepancy between visible engagement and ownership breadth and propose disclosure. Important methodological limitations: sample of repeat creators, cluster definitions may use full-history behavior, capped 100-signature wallet history is censored, holder analysis uses top-20 and drops holdings above 50% as protocol proxy (could discard a genuine controlling whale); no graduation-censoring adjustment; no causal or net-trading-return result. Do NOT inherit defaults: use point-in-time creator history, explicit coverage bounds, verified program-account exclusion not size cutoff, true holder-owner aggregation, actual event clocks, censoring and external validation. A 14-day wallet window is descriptive only, never a hard fraud gate. No claim that polished language equals AI, fraud or lack of potential.
- S2 https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01106 (Names without information, primary abstract inspected; full HTML unavailable; no full-method replication). Authors report 458,174 default-template tokens Feb–Jun 2026; outside buyer incidence rises 34.9% to 52.8% across appeal deciles but within creator and minute the effect is small and fails validation. Authors report no meaningful name-following return premium, declining inflow on reuse and faster copying after early capital success. Do not infer all narratives worthless; launchpad-specific observational paper, conditional-on-inflow result does not establish no total effect, full methodology and independent reproduction pending. Separate label/appeal discovery from net executable value and assess early capital as mediator rather than indiscriminate matching.
- S3 https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10246 sections 7.1–7.2 and 8.1–8.2 read. Already cited in merged #2212 Part06; incremental extraction only. Exact name/ticker/description/image match yields ~10% copycats in authors' sample; name/ticker only ~36% with false matches. Authors report first-created coins in exact-copy families graduated 9.20% vs all-token 1.02% and copies 0.86%; 17.7% of graduations are copies; only 0.40% of families had multiple graduates. Not a causal first-mover edge: retrospective family membership depends on later copies, earliest creation does not establish official origin, and authors break exact timestamp ties using later current market cap (prohibit for PIT labels). Same source with social behavior evidence: comments can be coordinated, positive and negative language both appear, timing/context more informative than tone alone. Their 1% comment sample and lockstep groups do not establish individual intent. For our own model use declared first-known ordered source events; unresolved ties remain ambiguous; never backfill family label onto observations before second token was detected. Include families with zero or one live member, genuine multi-chain, relaunches, common themes; audit source reach/coverage. No one-to-one copy of detector or claim of replications.
