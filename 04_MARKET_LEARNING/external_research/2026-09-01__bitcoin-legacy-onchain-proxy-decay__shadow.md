## Observation

Sminston argues that three historical Bitcoin adoption proxies — UTXOs, transaction count and addresses — have materially diverged from their pre-2018 power-law fits, while price remains close to its older power-law fit.

## Shadow hypothesis

The divergence may reflect **measurement decay rather than adoption decay**: custody, exchanges, ETFs, batching and off-chain/secondary rails can reduce the usefulness of raw addresses, transactions and UTXOs as direct adoption proxies as the network financializes.

Do **not** infer from this alone that price proves adoption still follows a power law. That remains unverified and may be circular.

## Research value

Potential future test: compare legacy on-chain proxies with a financialization-adjusted adoption stack combining economic settlement/activity, custody/exchange concentration, ETF ownership/flows, Lightning/L2 where measurable, realized-cap/entity-level metrics and transfer activity adjusted for churn.

Research question: **Has economic Bitcoin adoption remained structurally strong while legacy raw on-chain proxies lost representativeness?**
