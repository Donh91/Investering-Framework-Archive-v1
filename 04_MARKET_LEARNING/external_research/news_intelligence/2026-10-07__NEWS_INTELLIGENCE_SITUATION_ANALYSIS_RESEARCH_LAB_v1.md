# NEWS INTELLIGENCE & SITUATION ANALYSIS RESEARCH LAB v1

**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Status:** SHADOW_RESEARCH_ONLY_NON_CANONICAL  
**Authority:** NONE beyond research/context  
**Applies to:** Situation Room, external news, analyst warnings, macro headlines, policy communication, regulatory events, geopolitical events, crypto headlines, social-media discoveries

## 1. Mission

Improve the framework's interpretation of news without giving news direct market-state, portfolio, execution, Cycle Navigator, Master Monday, threshold or canonical authority.

The target question is not:

> Is this headline bullish or bearish?

The target question is:

> What verifiably happened, how surprising was it relative to expectations, through which channels should it transmit, what did markets actually do, did that reaction persist, and what would falsify the interpretation?

This mission deliberately preserves the existing Situation Room firewall:

DISCOVERY
-> PRIMARY / STRONG-INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION
-> REACTION OBSERVATION
-> TRANSMISSION HYPOTHESIS
-> PERSISTENCE / OUTCOME
-> LEARNING

Never:

DISCOVERY
-> HEADLINE POLARITY
-> TRADE

## 2. Existing framework alignment

## 3. Main research findings

### F1. Surprise matters more than headline direction

Classic event-study evidence shows that asset prices respond primarily to the unexpected component of macro announcements, not to the raw published number in isolation.

Operational implication:

A news item must distinguish:

- reported fact;
- prior consensus / market expectation;
- surprise direction;
- surprise magnitude where measurable;
- whether the information was already substantially priced.

A strong-sounding headline with low surprise can have low market information value.

Source:
- NBER, Pearce & Roley, "Stock Prices and Economic News": https://www.nber.org/papers/w1296

### F2. One announcement can contain multiple shocks

Federal Reserve research shows that monetary-policy events cannot be reduced to one "rate up/rate down" dimension. Current policy and the expected future path can affect different assets differently.

ECB research further shows that policy communication can contain both policy news and information about the central bank's economic outlook. A seemingly dovish decision can coincide with falling equities if the communication reveals worse economic information.

Operational implication:

For central-bank and policy news, split the event into at least:

- current-policy shock;
- future-path shock;
- economic-information shock;
- reaction-function / credibility shock when relevant.

Do not infer direction from the decision label alone.

Sources:
- Federal Reserve, Gürkaynak, Sack & Swanson: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/do-actions-speak-louder-than-words-the-response-of-asset-prices-to-monetary-policy-actions-and-statements.htm
- ECB, Jarociński & Karadi: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/research-publications/resbull/2018/html/ecb.rb180921.cs.html
- Federal Reserve, Knox & Vissing-Jorgensen, 2026: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/the-effect-of-the-federal-reserve-on-the-stock-market-magnitudes-channels-and-shocks.htm

### F3. The same macro news can change sign across regimes

NBER evidence on unemployment news shows that rising unemployment has historically been interpreted differently depending on the business-cycle state. During expansions, rate-relief effects can dominate. During contractions, earnings/cash-flow damage can dominate.

Operational implication:

No macro headline has a permanently fixed bullish/bearish sign.

News interpretation must include:

- current growth regime;
- inflation regime;
- policy regime;
- financial-stress regime;
- dominant market concern at T0.

Source:
- NBER, Boyd, Jagannathan & Hu: https://www.nber.org/papers/w8092

### F4. Cross-asset footprint helps identify what the market heard

Different components of a policy surprise leave different footprints across:

- short rates;
- long rates;
- equities;
- FX;
- credit;
- commodities.

This makes cross-asset reaction more informative than a single equity or crypto move.

Operational implication:

A significant event should be read as a footprint, not one ticker:

RATES
+ USD
+ EQUITIES
+ CREDIT / VOL
+ OIL / GOLD where relevant
+ BTC
+ ETH
+ ETH/BTC
+ crypto breadth

The goal is not to force all assets to agree. The goal is to identify the likely shock family.

Sources:
- Federal Reserve, Global Asset Prices and FOMC Announcements: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/ifdp/global-asset-prices-and-fomc-announcements.htm
- ECB, monetary-policy signals: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/research-publications/resbull/2020/html/ecb.rb200722~528ea64f0d.en.html

### F5. Reaction must be compared with background volatility

ECB event-study work uses narrow before/after windows and compares moves with ordinary background volatility. This prevents normal market noise from being mislabelled as event impact.

Operational implication:

For scheduled or timestamped events, classify reaction magnitude relative to the prevailing volatility regime:

- IMMATERIAL;
- MATERIAL;
- EXTREME.

Do not equate "price moved after headline" with "headline moved price".

Source:
- ECB, 2026 communication event study: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/blog/date/2026/html/ecb.blog20260924~3240af33dc.en.html

### F6. Communication itself can be market-moving

Policy speeches and interviews can have market effects comparable to formal decisions. Therefore a Situation Room style system must distinguish:

- formal action;
- official guidance;
- prepared speech;
- interview;
- offhand remark;
- analyst opinion;
- media interpretation.

The speaker's institutional authority and whether the communication changes expected future policy matter.

Source:
- ECB, 2026 communication event study: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/blog/date/2026/html/ecb.blog20260924~3240af33dc.en.html

### F7. Discovery source is not evidence source

The SEC documents cases where false social-media posts caused immediate large price moves. In January 2024, the compromised official SEC X account falsely announced spot Bitcoin ETF approval. The SEC later documented a brief roughly $1,000 / 2.5% Bitcoin spike.

Operational implication:

Even an apparently official social-media account can be false.

For government/regulatory action, the preferred proof is the agency's authoritative publication surface, filing, order, release, or equivalent primary record.

Situation Room, WatcherGuru, X, Telegram and similar surfaces are radar/discovery sources unless independently verified.

Sources:
- SEC, unauthorized access statement: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/gensler-x-account
- SEC, SECGov X Account incident page: https://www.sec.gov/secgov-x-account
- SEC OIG market-impact note in 2024 management challenges: https://www.sec.gov/files/statement-secs-management-performance-challenges-october-2024.pdf
- SEC false-tweet market-manipulation case: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2015-254

### F8. Market reaction can be more informative than the narrative

When the market receives apparently bad news and refuses to fall, or receives apparently good news and fails to rally, the divergence is information.

Examples of useful reaction classes:

- BAD_NEWS_ABSORBED;
- GOOD_NEWS_REJECTED;
- INITIAL_MOVE_REVERSED;
- CROSS_ASSET_CONFIRMATION;
- CROSS_ASSET_DIVERGENCE;
- NO_MATERIAL_REACTION.

This is not proof of future direction. It is evidence about positioning, expectations and regime sensitivity.

### F9. Immediate reaction is not always the full reaction

Research on limited investor attention and post-announcement drift shows that public information can sometimes be incorporated gradually.

Operational implication:

The framework should not close a news case after the first candle.

Evaluate at multiple horizons:

- T+15m / T+90m when timestamp quality allows;
- T+4h;
- T+24h;
- T+3d;
- T+7d;
- T+30d only for structural / slow hypotheses.

Sources:
- NBER, Investor Inattention and Friday Earnings Announcements: https://www.nber.org/papers/w11683
- NBER review on analyst/investor behavioral biases: https://www.nber.org/reporter-2020-02/behavioral-biases-analysts-and-investors

### F10. Geopolitical news requires threat-vs-realization separation

Federal Reserve research distinguishes geopolitical threats from realized adverse events and shows that geopolitical risk is associated with downside risks to economic activity. Exposure also varies by industry and institution.

Operational implication:

A geopolitical headline needs separate states:

- THREAT / RHETORIC;
- POLICY ACTION;
- REALIZED MILITARY / ECONOMIC EVENT;
- ESCALATION;
- DE-ESCALATION.

Then map actual channels such as oil, shipping, sanctions, funding, credit and risk appetite.

Sources:
- Federal Reserve, Caldara & Iacoviello geopolitical risk research: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/ifdp/2018.htm
- Federal Reserve, bank-stock response to geopolitical risk, 2026: https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/how-u-s-bank-stock-prices-respond-to-geopolitical-risk-20260602.html

This is a research object, not a new engine.

Required fields:

### Identity
- EVENT_ID
- EVENT_FAMILY_ID
- DETECTION_TIME
- EVENT_TIME
- SOURCE_URL
- SOURCE_ROLE

### Verification
- FACT_STATUS: VERIFIED / PARTIAL / UNVERIFIED / FALSE

### Event semantics
- EVENT_TYPE
- ACTION_STATE: RUMOR / STATEMENT / INTENTION / PROPOSAL / DECISION / IMPLEMENTATION / REALIZED_EVENT
- NOVELTY: LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH
- EXPECTATION_STATE: EXPECTED / PARTLY_EXPECTED / SURPRISE / UNKNOWN

## 5. News-processing sequence

### Stage A - Discovery

Question:
What was reported?

No interpretation privilege.

### Stage B - Verification

Question:
What can be proven by primary or strong independent evidence?

If not verified:
PENDING or FALSE.

### Stage C - Surprise decomposition

Question:
What part was new relative to expectations?

For unscheduled analyst opinions:
the "surprise" is not the opinion's negativity/positivity, but whether it introduces new evidence, new positioning information or a new falsifiable thesis.

### Stage D - Transmission map

Question:
If the thesis matters, what should move first, second and later?

Every material interpretation must state expected sequence before outcome is known.

### Stage E - Reaction observation

Question:
What actually moved, by how much relative to normal volatility, and did related assets agree?

### Stage F - Persistence

Question:
Did the footprint survive or reverse?

### Stage G - Outcome / learning

Question:
Was the initial interpretation useful?

Store both:
- false warnings;
- missed warnings.

## 6. Historical calibration cases

These are archetypes, not claimed framework track record.

### Case H1 - Federal Reserve target vs path

Lesson:
"Fed action" is multidimensional. Current-rate and future-path surprises affect assets differently.

Use:
central-bank decomposition.

Evidence:
Federal Reserve Gürkaynak/Sack/Swanson study.

### Case H2 - ECB 4 Aug 2011 information shock

Observed in ECB research:
policy looked looser than expected, rates fell, yet equities also fell as the announcement conveyed negative economic information.

Lesson:
"Dovish = bullish equities" is invalid without information-shock analysis.

### Case H3 - Unemployment news sign flip

NBER evidence:
higher unemployment can be equity-positive in expansions through expected rate relief, and equity-negative in contractions through earnings damage.

Lesson:
headline polarity must be regime conditional.

### Case H4 - Policy speeches and interviews

ECB 2026 evidence:
inter-meeting communication can create significant market reactions.

Lesson:
formal decision vs informal communication is a source-authority distinction, not an assumption that speeches are irrelevant.

### Case H5 - SEC false ETF approval post, 9 Jan 2024

Verified:
SEC X account compromised; false spot-Bitcoin-ETF approval posted; SEC later documented a brief approximately 2.5% Bitcoin spike.

Lesson:
a market reaction does not verify the claim that caused it.

### Case H6 - Actual spot Bitcoin ETP approval, 10 Jan 2024

Verified:
SEC approved spot Bitcoin ETP listings the next day.

Lesson:
TRUE/FALSE and BULLISH/BEARISH are separate dimensions. A true event can be substantially anticipated, while a false event can move price sharply.

Primary source:
https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/gensler-statement-spot-bitcoin-011023

### Case H7 - Social-media stock rumor manipulation

SEC enforcement examples show false social posts can trigger large price changes and later reversals.

Lesson:
price confirmation is not source confirmation.

### Case H8 - Geopolitical threat vs realization

Federal Reserve GPR work shows threat and realized events both matter, but transmission depends on exposure.

Lesson:
separate rhetoric/threat from realized channels.

### Case H9 - Investor inattention / delayed reaction

NBER evidence shows slower incorporation around lower-attention announcement environments.

Lesson:
do not assume first reaction is final reaction.

## 7. Forward test v1

### Objective

Test whether NEWS EVIDENCE CARD v1 adds predictive/diagnostic value beyond simple headline reading.

### Baselines

**B0 - Headline Polarity**
Bullish / bearish based only on headline language.

**B1 - Verified Fact + Immediate Price**
Primary/strong verification plus immediate market direction.

**B2 - News Evidence Card**
Verification + surprise + regime + transmission + cross-asset reaction + persistence.

### Prospective sample

Minimum before promotion:
- 50 prospectively frozen events;
- at least 10 macro/policy;
- at least 10 crypto/regulatory;
- at least 10 geopolitical/energy;
- at least 10 corporate/market-structure/analyst warnings;
- remainder unrestricted.

No category may dominate more than 40%.

### Evaluation horizons

Default:
- 4h
- 24h
- 3d
- 7d

Structural hypotheses may add 30d / 90d / 180d, declared at T0.

### Metrics

- verified-fact accuracy;
- false-source admission rate;
- false-escalation rate;
- material-event miss rate;
- directional usefulness by horizon;
- confidence calibration;
- time-to-correction after wrong initial classification;
- causal-overreach count;
- whether B2 beats B0 and B1 on material-event discrimination;
- whether B2 improves Situation Room context without increasing false alarms.

### Promotion condition

Remain research-only unless B2 shows useful improvement over baselines on a sufficiently large prospective sample.

No promotion based on anecdotal wins.

### Kill / simplify condition

Simplify or retire NEWS EVIDENCE CARD elements if, after 50 prospective events:

- they do not improve discrimination versus B1;
- they materially increase false escalation;
- they cannot be populated reliably;
- they create subjective labels with low inter-reviewer consistency;
- or they add narrative complexity without measurable outcome value.

## 8. Special handling for analyst / investor warnings

Analyst warnings, including Michael Burry-style crash calls, are hypotheses, not catalysts.

Classify as:

`EXTERNAL_MARKET_HYPOTHESIS`

Required treatment:

1. verify that the person actually made the claim;
2. extract the falsifiable thesis;
3. separate timing from direction;
4. list required transmission channels;
5. list falsifiers;
6. freeze the horizon;
7. observe outcome prospectively;
8. never let reputation substitute for evidence.

For a "crash is coming" claim, useful transmission tests may include:

- equity breadth;
- leadership concentration;
- credit spreads / funding stress;
- Treasury-yield regime;
- earnings breadth;
- volatility;
- small-cap participation;
- BTC structure;
- ETH/BTC;
- crypto breadth / deployment.

## 10. Final synthesis

The central upgrade is:

OLD:
headline -> sentiment -> market opinion

NEW:
discovery
-> verification
-> expectation/surprise
-> regime-conditioned transmission hypothesis
-> cross-asset reaction
-> persistence
-> falsification
-> outcome learning

The strongest new principle is:

**A headline is not evidence. A price move is not verification. A verified event is not automatically surprising. A surprising event is not automatically directional. And an initial reaction is not necessarily the final transmission.**

That is the analytical discipline this research mission adds.
