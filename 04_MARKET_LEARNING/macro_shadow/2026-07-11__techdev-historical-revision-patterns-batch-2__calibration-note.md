The new sources close nearly the entire Full Issue sequence from #18 through #60. ### 2. Invalidation drift is visible and must remain auditable

Several concrete price and indicator gates were introduced, failed or weakened, while the directional thesis survived through:

- moving from daily supports to weekly supports;
- changing the comparison period;
- lowering the hard invalidation;
- changing corrective-wave labels;
- replacing short analogs with higher-timeframe analogs.

The framework must therefore preserve each original gate and each later revision as separate rows. A later useful model cannot retroactively repair an earlier claim.

### 3. Mechanical systems and analyst discretion were not consistently separated

The 2D and 3D signal systems were presented with fixed rules and strong backtest claims, but the source chain also contains:

- rapid parameter and state-rule changes;
- missed-profit and exit-flaw acknowledgements;
- new take-profit rules;
- later overrides of mechanical exits when the macro accumulation interpretation disagreed.

Any future test must distinguish mechanical signal outcome from discretionary override outcome.

### 4. Confirmation often arrived after substantial price movement

The March-April 2023 confirmation stack was explicitly framed as high confidence, while the author also noted that waiting for it meant entering after BTC had already risen roughly 80-90% from the lows and some alts had multiplied. Confirmation quality and action timeliness therefore require separate scores.

### 5. Correlated confluence should not be counted as independent evidence

Alt cap, BTC.D, ETH/BTC, DXY, USDT.D, equities breadth, CN10Y/DXY and risk-on measures can share common liquidity and risk-appetite drivers. Their agreement may be informative, but raw indicator count would exaggerate independence.

### 6. Target methodology improved through explicit revision

The archive records a real methodological improvement from price-only Fibonacci targets toward market-cap-aware targets when supply inflation matters, followed by a further shift toward relative-strength classification where young altcoin charts do not support reliable wave counts.
