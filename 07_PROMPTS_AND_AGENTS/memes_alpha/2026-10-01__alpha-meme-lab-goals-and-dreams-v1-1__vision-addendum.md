## Why this goal is being added

BYTE and the Wazz serial-extraction cases strengthened a new top-level Alpha Lab ambition:

> Do not merely identify the wallet that launched a prior token. Learn to recognize the next operator's preparation even when the operator rotates to fresh wallets.

The operator can cheaply replace an address.

It is materially harder to replace the entire behavioral sequence around a coordinated launch:

`funding -> quote-asset preparation -> residual-balance pattern -> approvals/relaunch attempts -> synchronized inventory formation -> coordinated distribution`.

Alpha Lab should therefore learn to recognize repeatable economic behavior across changing addresses, while explicitly removing benign infrastructure such as Relay, bridges, routers, CEX paths, faucets and shared protocol contracts.

## New goal H — Adversarial Operator Anticipation

Long-run aspiration:

> Detect a previously extractive or highly coordinated operator's next launch preparation early enough to create a time-sensitive review before broad social propagation, even when every visible wallet is new.

Desired capability:

1. maintain point-in-time economic-entity memory across prior launches;
2. distinguish operator-specific edges from shared infrastructure;
3. identify fresh/dormant wallet cohorts that wake up in a narrow prelaunch window;
4. detect common quote-asset preparation and unusual residual-balance similarity;
5. recognize repeated launch / relaunch / approval behavior;
6. infer coordinated beneficial inventory formation without equating every linked wallet with one real-world person;
7. compare the emerging launch against prior extraction families such as BYTE and the Wazz historical operator set;
8. alert for `TIME_SENSITIVE_REVIEW_NOW` only when the evidence is visible prospectively.

The goal is not to identify or deanonymize people.

The unit of analysis is the economic/operator cluster and its observable on-chain behavior.

## New goal I — Realizable First-Pump / Adversarial-Alpha Window

Some launches connected to extractive operators may still produce a short, public and sellable expansion before coordinated distribution overwhelms independent demand.

Alpha Lab should research whether that window can be detected and measured without hindsight.

The research question is:

> Can public on-chain preparation expose the operator before broad buyers arrive, and can the system distinguish a genuinely realizable early expansion window from merely becoming the operator's exit liquidity?

This is deliberately dual-axis:

`HIGH EXTRACTION RISK` does not imply `ZERO SHORT-TERM MFE`.

`LARGE MFE` does not imply `REALIZABLE OR SAFE ALPHA`.

The system must separately measure:
- operator/extraction risk;
- public-eligible entry time;
- independent buyer breadth;
- liquidity and bounded-notional sellability;
- operator inventory and sell-through;
- time to first coordinated distribution;
- realizable MFE after fees/slippage;
- MAE;
- exit-window duration;
- execution toxicity / MEV burden.

## "Outsmart the extractors" research framing

The informal aspiration is to "outsmart the scammers".

The scientific formulation is narrower:

> Determine whether known extractive operators expose a repeatable public on-chain preparation pattern that can be observed before their expected distribution phase, and whether a bounded early-exit policy would have positive prospective expectancy after failed launches, non-sellable cases, slippage, gas, false positives and missed winners are included.

This may fail.

It must not become a strategy merely because historical examples look attractive.

## Exit-policy research, not trade instruction

A future shadow backtest may compare bounded exit policies such as:

- fixed T+30s / T+2m / T+5m / T+15m exits;
- first verified operator-distribution event;
- first collapse in independent buyer breadth;
- first liquidity/sellability deterioration;
- a simple baseline such as no-entry / fixed-time exit.

Every result must include:
- first public executable timestamp;
- realistic slippage;
- gas/fees;
- bounded notional;
- failed / reverted / unsellable paths;
- latency;
- missed entries;
- maximum adverse excursion before exit.

No policy may receive credit from an unreachable ATH.

## Prospective success condition

This goal earns promotion only if future frozen rows show that:

1. the preparation fingerprint is observable before or at T0, not reconstructed later;
2. operator-specific evidence survives removal of benign infrastructure;
3. matched controls do not show the same pattern at similar rates;
4. the signal adds incremental lead time or discrimination beyond existing Project->CA / Moonshot owners;
5. a public-eligible, bounded-notional exit window is actually executable;
6. false positives, severe drawdowns and unsellable cases remain acceptable;
7. the result survives enough distinct prospective cases to justify a separate governance review.

The added ambition is to make Alpha Lab harder to fool specifically when the apparent opportunity is being manufactured by sophisticated economic actors who rotate wallets and prepare inventory before the public launch.

The ideal future result is not merely:

> "this looks like a rug."
